OVERALL REPORT / DRAFT



Abstract

 We tested three parts of a NoLA-style adaptation pipeline: CLIP class-description embeddings (CDE),
 pseudo-labeled anchor selection and visual retrieval, and visual-prompt student training. The strongest
 reproducible-within-run effect of a modification was targeted rescue of an underfilled EuroSAT class: direct
 DINO retrieval raised Herbaceous Vegetation recall from 2/600 without rescue to 98/600, compared with 72/600
 for a matched CLIP expansion. Overall gains were smaller and did not consistently transfer. Replacing CLIP
 with SigLIP 2 for independent anchor selection clearly degraded the EuroSAT DINO teacher under both
 retrievers. An early EuroSAT win for the SigLIP 2 + ConvNeXt student disappeared at longer fixed training
 budgets. We have not established that any modification improves on the release-style CLIP baseline in a
 matched, full-split student test, or across datasets.

Protocol and baseline

 CDE denotes the frozen text-based classifier; the teacher is a learned head on frozen original DINO ViT-B/16
 features; the student is CLIP or SigLIP 2 with visual-prompt adaptation. DINOv3 ConvNeXt-Tiny
 (convnext_tiny.dinov3_lvd1689m) was a frozen retrieval encoder only, never the teacher or student. Most
 follow-ups use development validation, with one training seed per major comparison. Coarse CIFAR-100 includes
 separate 5,000-image validation analyses and a 1,024-image simulated-scarcity teacher pilot; these are not
 interchangeable. RESISC45 uses an adapter-derived split. The older frozen Kaggle/DNC study was not re-audited
 and is outside this draft.



 The local release-style EuroSAT run achieved 51.09% CDE, 67.09% DINO teacher, and 76.69% best student on the
 same 5,400 validation images. The validation-selected epoch-3 checkpoint scored 76.27% on 8,100 test images
 (6,178 correct). The same run finished at 71.13% validation at epoch 50. A small sweep with 160 and 320
 adaptation images showed that a larger anchor cap could improve the teacher while hurting the student; only 10
 of 128 jobs from the larger hyperparameter grid were saved. These are reasons to record class coverage and
 checkpoint trajectories, not evidence that longer training or larger anchor budgets always hurt.

Prompt and prototype hypotheses



 A 240-image EuroSAT pilot favored three prototypes with log-mean-exp (127 correct) over one mean (118); max
 pooling scored 103. The larger 5,160-image analysis showed that clustering the authored bank could
 underperform both the single mean (53.59%) and the unmerged bank (54.28%): merged variants scored
 48.39–49.42%. Broad-plus-variant prompts improved the weak broad-only bank (47.67% to 50.21% at best) but did
 not beat those controls. For the released prompt bank, one merge reached 52.29% versus 51.10% for released
 CDE, yet Herbaceous stayed at zero correct. The SVD/cosine audit showed that dimensionality reduction could
 make prompt pairs appear close that did not pass the full-space test. Photo-wrapped prototypes on EuroSAT
 briefly improved the student by 1.15 pp at epoch 1 and lost 0.30 pp at epoch 2; on RESISC45 the frozen score
 declined from 58.03% to 56.05%. Conclusion: neither prompt grouping nor a better raw text score guarantees
 useful anchors or a better student.

Anchor and retrieval hypotheses



 The baseline sometimes nominated no correct candidates for a class. For example, a small EuroSAT audit found
 24 true Herbaceous fit images, zero first-choice CLIP nominations, and one incorrect fallback. More anchors
 helped only if the additional images were useful. Farthest-first and medoids lowered teacher accuracy on all
 three datasets. Neighbor agreement improved EuroSAT by +2.67 pp [1.80, 3.54], was inconclusive on RESISC45
 (+0.27 pp [−0.59, 1.12]), and hurt coarse CIFAR-100 (−1.18 pp [−2.20, −0.18]). Forced cluster balance lost
 roughly 9.50–11.24 pp on EuroSAT; mapping text prototypes onto clusters did not recover the loss.



 In the first cluster-first rescue, all 113 additions went to Pasture and none to Herbaceous. Opening the pool,
 while matching 64 additions per target class, changed that: no-rescue, CLIP expansion, and direct DINO
 retrieval gave 2/600, 72/600, and 98/600 Herbaceous recall. Direct retrieval raised overall teacher top-1 from
 67.28% to 68.02% versus matched CLIP expansion (+0.74 pp [0.15, 1.35]) and selected 96/128 correct additions
 versus 79/128. DBSCAN reached 68.28%, but one dominant cluster and thousands of noise points do not establish
 meaningful subtypes. Ward and spherical k-means did not surpass DBSCAN in this run; spectral clustering and
 CLIP-reweighted edges did not consistently beat direct retrieval. In the CIFAR scarcity pilot, direct DINO had
 38/40 correct additions versus CLIP's 28/40, yet lowered teacher accuracy, showing that anchor purity alone is
 an insufficient criterion.

 observed CIFAR run was CLIP + DINO at epoch 2 (60.50%). Best-observed validation scores are descriptive and
 cannot be compared directly with the 76.27% full-split EuroSAT test baseline.

Conclusions and next comparison



 Keep released CLIP CDE and the simple confidence selector as the baseline. Test targeted expansion for
 genuinely underfilled classes, using CLIP-only expansion as the control that distinguishes the added budget
 from the retrieval method. Compare against direct DINO retrieval and optionally ConvNeXt with the same
 per-class additions. Use EuroSAT and a second dataset with measured natural scarcity; keep the student
 architecture and checkpoint-selection rule fixed, report multiple seeds, and lock a held-out test before
 looking at outcomes. Report overall student top-1, correctness and coverage of rescued anchors, rare-class
 recall, and any regression on non-target classes. The current runs support a rescue hypothesis, not a
 universal new pipeline.



 Statistical notes: differences are in percentage points; quoted 95% intervals are paired image-level bootstrap
 intervals from the report builders or archived studies. The recent frozen comparisons stratify by true class
 (fine subtype for CIFAR), and retain exact McNemar p-values and fix/loss counts in the PDF or machine-readable
 results. Repeated development sets, multiple comparisons, training seeds, and checkpoint selection limit
 generalization; the intervals do not quantify all of those sources of uncertainty. No new experiments were run
 to write this draft.

APPENDIX / EXPERIMENT INVENTORY


Baseline, scale, and what fails
Hypotheses, measured results, and failure modes; stages and splits are kept separate.



 HYPOTHESIS
 EuroSAT small pipeline and 160/320-image sweep: more data or more anchors should help.

 RESULT
 160-image trace: 35 candidates → 34 distinct anchors, 25 correct; Herbaceous had no CLIP top-1 candidates and
 needed fallback. In the separate four-cell sweep, CDE was 117/240, DINO 142–159/240, student 81–140/240.
 Raising the cap from 4 to 12 added anchors, reduced precision, improved the teacher in both matched rows, and
 hurt the student. At 320 images one class lost coverage.

 INTERPRETATION
 More images or anchors do not guarantee class coverage; fallback and deduplication matter. Higher teacher
 accuracy did not automatically reach the student.


 HYPOTHESIS
 Larger small-data hyperparameter grid: tune head and student to remove the gap.

 RESULT
 10 of 128 configurations are saved, 20 epoch checkpoints. Best saved student 143/240, tied with its teacher;
 students ranged from 59 to 143 correct.

 INTERPRETATION
 Partial, single-seed grid; no defensible global winner. Epochs and batch size also change the number of
 updates.


 HYPOTHESIS
 Instrumented EuroSAT CPU replay and DINO-space audit: find the specific failure.

 RESULT
 On 240 validation images: CDE 117, teacher 134, student 87 at epoch 1 and 124 at epoch 4. Fresh 240-image
 subset: 130, 137, and 121 at epoch 4. Herbaceous: 24 true train examples, zero top-1 nominations, one wrong
 fallback, 0/16 validation correct throughout. Pasture had no correct anchors; Permanent Crop had four wrong
 anchors. Two of nine supported predicted-class DINO regions lacked anchors; only 3/7 and 1/4 of their rows had
 correct CLIP labels.

 INTERPRETATION
 An absent nomination is different from low anchor precision. Pure diversity may give more slots to coherent
 *wrong* labels. At epoch 4 the student fixed 24 teacher errors and lost 34 correct teacher predictions.


 HYPOTHESIS
 Full-split EuroSAT local release-style baseline: is NoLA itself viable at scale?

 RESULT
 CDE 51.09%, teacher 67.09%, best student 76.69% validation (epoch 3); selected checkpoint 76.27% test
 (6,178/8,100). Epoch 50 fell to 71.13% validation.

 INTERPRETATION
 End-to-end NoLA worked well in this run. Checkpoint selection mattered; small pilots that lost to DINO do not
 establish that the student generally fails. Local release-style run, not an exact multi-seed paper
 reproduction.

Current judgment and next hypothesis
Hypotheses, measured results, and failure modes; stages and splits are kept separate.



 1. Keep the released single-mean CDE and confidence-based anchors as the cross-dataset baseline. Do not
 promote a template, threshold, or clustering rule just because it won one development run.


 2. Most promising mechanism: a class needs correct anchors across its visual modes. Missing nominations cannot
 be fixed by reranking the same candidates, and adding correct but redundant anchors can still harm
 generalization. Measure candidate availability, correct anchors per class/subtype, teacher accuracy, and
 student accuracy independently.



 3. Independent replacement is now tested: see siglip-replacement/README.md. At matched budgets, SigLIP 2-only
 teacher accuracy trails CLIP-only by 9.89 pp on EuroSAT and by 1.17 pp on the CIFAR-100 pilot (the latter
 interval includes zero). SigLIP nominates 144 correct EuroSAT Herbaceous examples at top-1 versus none from
 CLIP, yet selects fewer correct Herbaceous anchors with the matched budgets. Keep CLIP as default; the next
 discriminating check is how to rank candidates for that class, rather than another wholesale backbone swap. A
 secondary check is satellite suffix mean versus released CDE at matched anchor budgets.



 Technical issues (not scientific failures): an import namespace collision in a local pilot was worked around
 before the successful EuroSAT run; public archive transfers timed out/reset, and the RESISC45 archive was
 resumed and hash-checked. A float32 grouping consistency check differed by 1.69e-7; moving the check to
 float64 recovered the run without changing classifier math. No score was inferred for unfinished runs.
